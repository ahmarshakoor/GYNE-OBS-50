50 High-Yield Topics - Obstetrics & Gynaecology

Complete master review list (Topics 1–50) with standardized clinical features, diagnostic pearls, and expanded treatment details.

High-Yield Obstetrics (Pregnancy & Labor)

001 Ectopic Pregnancy >30 times
Implantation of a fertilized ovum outside the uterine cavity. Most commonly occurs in the ampulla of the fallopian tube.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Triad: Amenorrhea, lower abdominal pain, and vaginal bleeding.
  • Palpable adnexal mass on pelvic exam.
  • Signs of rupture: Sudden severe pain, hemorrhagic shock, peritonitis.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Serum β-hCG level.
  • Diagnostic Imaging: Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) showing empty uterine cavity and adnexal mass.
Treatment
  • 1st Line (Medical): Methotrexate for hemodynamically stable, unruptured cases.
  • Definitive/Surgical: Laparoscopic Salpingectomy or Salpingostomy for ruptured or unstable cases.
002 Preeclampsia & Eclampsia >20 times
New-onset hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) and proteinuria after 20 weeks of gestation. Leading cause of maternal morbidity.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Headache, visual disturbances, epigastric/RUQ pain.
  • Eclampsia Triad: Hypertension + Proteinuria + Grand mal seizures.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Urine dipstick (≥1+).
  • Diagnostic Test: 24-hour urine collection showing ≥300 mg proteinuria (Gold Standard).
Treatment
  • 1st Line BP Control: IV Labetalol, IV Hydralazine, or Oral Nifedipine.
  • Seizure Control/Prevention: IV Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO4).
  • Definitive Treatment: Delivery of the fetus.
003 Placenta Previa >15 times
Placenta implants in the lower uterine segment, partially or completely covering the internal cervical os.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Presentation: Sudden, painless, bright red vaginal bleeding in the 3rd trimester.
  • Soft, non-tender uterus.
  • Digital vaginal examination is strictly contraindicated.
Diagnosis
  • Diagnostic Test: Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) to precisely locate placental tissue relative to the os.
Treatment
  • 1st Line / Conservative: Expectant management and pelvic rest if stable and <37 weeks.
  • Definitive Treatment: Cesarean Section delivery.
004 Placental Abruption (Abruptio Placentae) >10 times
Premature separation of a normally implanted placenta from the uterine wall before birth. Strongly associated with hypertension and trauma.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Triad: Painful, tender uterus + Dark red vaginal bleeding + High-frequency uterine contractions.
Diagnosis
  • Diagnostic Test: Clinical Diagnosis supported by Ultrasound (primarily to rule out placenta previa).
Treatment
  • 1st Line: Hemodynamic stabilization, fluid resuscitation, and blood transfusion.
  • Definitive Treatment: Immediate emergency Cesarean Section.
005 Postpartum Hemorrhage (Uterine Atony) >15 times
Failure of the uterus to effectively contract following delivery, accounting for ~80% of PPH cases.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Profuse vaginal bleeding following delivery (>500 mL for NSVD, >1000 mL for C-section).
  • Physical findings show a soft, enlarged, and boggy uterus on palpation.
Diagnosis
  • Diagnostic Step: Bimanual pelvic examination.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Treatment: Bimanual uterine massage + IV Oxytocin.
  • Adjunct/2nd Line: Ergometrine, Misoprostol, or Carboprost.
  • Definitive/Surgical: Uterine balloon tamponade, uterine artery embolization, or surgical ligation/hysterectomy.
006 Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) >8 times
Carbohydrate intolerance resulting in hyperglycemia with onset or first recognition during pregnancy. Routine screening occurs at 24–28 weeks.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Usually asymptomatic; may present with excessive maternal weight gain, polyhydramnios, or a large-for-gestational-age (macrosomic) fetus.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: 1-hour Oral Glucose Challenge Test (OGCT).
  • Confirmatory Diagnostic Test: 3-hour Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT).
Treatment
  • 1st Line: Lifestyle modifications, physical activity, and dietary adjustments.
  • Pharmacotherapy (Definitive Medical): Subcutaneous Insulin therapy (does not cross the placenta).
007 Rh Incompatibility >5 times
Maternal sensitization occurring when an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive fetus, leading to isoimmunization.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Fetal/Neonatal features: Severe hemolytic anemia, jaundice, kernicterus, and hydrops fetalis.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Screening: Maternal Blood Type and Indirect Coombs test for anti-D antibodies.
  • Diagnostic/Monitoring: Middle Cerebral Artery (MCA) Doppler ultrasound to detect fetal anemia.
Treatment & Prophylaxis
  • Prophylaxis (1st Line): Anti-D Immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) given at 28 weeks gestation and within 72 hours of delivery.
  • Definitive Fetal Management: Intrauterine blood transfusion via umbilical vein if severe anemia occurs.
008 Antihypertensives in Pregnancy >8 times
Management of chronic or gestational hypertension with strict focus on drug safety profiles.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
First-Line Safe Drugs
  • Labetalol: Combined alpha/beta blocker (1st line).
  • Nifedipine: Calcium channel blocker.
  • Alpha-methyldopa: Central alpha-2 agonist (traditional option).
Contraindicated Drugs
  • ACE inhibitors & ARBs (e.g., Captopril, Enalapril) are strictly contraindicated due to risk of fetal renal dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, and death.
Clinical Goal
  • Maintain BP to prevent maternal stroke while avoiding acute reductions that compromise uteroplacental perfusion.
009 Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA) in Pregnancy >12 times
Microcytic hypochromic anemia caused by expanded maternal plasma volume and increased fetal iron demands. Most common cause of anemia in pregnancy.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Fatigue, lethargy, pallor, dyspnea, and pica.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: CBC showing low Hb (<11 g/dL in 1st trimester, <10.5 g/dL in 2nd) and low MCV.
  • Best Diagnostic Investigation: Serum Ferritin (low level confirms diagnosis).
Treatment
  • 1st Line: Oral Ferrous Sulfate supplementation.
  • Definitive/Severe Cases: IV Iron sucrose or blood transfusion if near term or severely symptomatic.
010 Folic Acid & Neural Tube Defects (NTDs) >10 times
Vitamin B9 deficiency during early neurulation resulting in failure of neural tube closure (e.g., spina bifida, anencephaly).
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Manifestations
  • Anencephaly (absence of major portion of brain/skull).
  • Spina Bifida (myelomeningocele, meningocele) causing motor deficits below lesion.
Diagnosis
  • Screening Test: Maternal serum Alpha-fetoprotein (MSAFP) at 15–20 weeks.
  • Diagnostic Imaging: High-resolution Targeted Ultrasound.
Prophylaxis & Management
  • Standard Prophylaxis: Folic Acid 400 mcg/day starting 1 month prior to conception.
  • High-Risk Dosage: Folic Acid 4 mg/day for women with prior NTD child, diabetes, or taking anti-epileptic drugs.

High-Yield Gynaecology (Reproductive Health & Oncology)

011 Endometriosis >20 times
Presence of functional endometrial tissue (glands and stroma) outside the uterine cavity.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Triad: Dysmenorrhea + Dyspareunia + Infertility.
  • Chronic pelvic pain and dyschezia.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Imaging: Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) showing "chocolate cysts" (endometriomas).
  • Gold Standard Diagnostic: Diagnostic Laparoscopy with biopsy.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Medical: NSAIDs for pain; Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCPs), Progestins, or GnRH agonists.
  • Definitive Surgical: Laparoscopic ablation/excision of implants, or Hysterectomy with BSO.
012 Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) >15 times
Complex endocrine disorder characterized by hyperandrogenism, ovulatory dysfunction, and metabolic disturbances.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Rotterdam Criteria (2 of 3): Oligo/anovulation, clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism (hirsutism, acne), polycystic ovaries.
  • Associated with insulin resistance and obesity.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Pelvic Ultrasound showing "string of pearls" peripheral follicles.
  • Lab Findings: LH:FSH ratio (>2:1), serum testosterone.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Intervention: Weight loss and lifestyle modifications.
  • Medical: COCPs for cycle regulation/hirsutism; Metformin for insulin resistance; Clomiphene or Letrozole for ovulation induction.
013 Uterine Fibroids (Leiomyomas) >12 times
Benign smooth muscle tumors originating from the myometrium. Most common benign pelvic tumor in females.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia), pelvic pressure, frequency of urination, enlarged asymmetric non-tender uterus.
Diagnosis
  • Best Initial Test: Pelvic Ultrasound showing well-circumscribed hypoechoic masses.
  • Most Sensitive Test: Pelvic MRI for precise mapping prior to surgery.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Medical: Tranexamic acid, COCPs, LNG-IUS, or GnRH agonists.
  • Surgical Management: Myomectomy (to preserve fertility) or Hysterectomy (definitive).
014 Cervical Cancer >12 times
Malignancy arising from the cervical transformation zone, strongly linked to high-risk HPV types 16 and 18.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Hallmark symptom: Post-coital vaginal bleeding.
  • Malodorous discharge, pelvic pain in advanced stages.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Screening: Pap Smear cytology + High-risk HPV co-testing.
  • Confirmatory Diagnostic Test: Colposcopy with directed punch biopsy.
Treatment
  • Early Stage: LEEP, cold-knife conization, or radical hysterectomy.
  • Advanced Stage: Concurrent chemoradiation (Cisplatin-based).
015 Endometrial Carcinoma >10 times
Malignancy of the endometrial lining, most commonly adenocarcinoma, preceded by atypical endometrial hyperplasia.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Classic presentation: Postmenopausal vaginal bleeding.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Imaging: Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) showing endometrial thickness >4 mm.
  • Gold Standard Diagnostic Test: Endometrial Biopsy (Pipelle).
Treatment
  • Definitive Surgical Treatment: Total Abdominal Hysterectomy with Bilateral Salpingo-Oophorectomy (TAH-BSO) and pelvic lymphadenectomy.
016 Types of Miscarriage (Abortions) >12 times
Pregnancy loss occurring before 20 weeks of gestation or fetal weight <500g.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Classifications
  • Threatened: Bleeding, closed os, positive fetal cardiac activity.
  • Incomplete: Bleeding, dilated os, partial expulsion of tissue.
  • Missed: Fetal death without tissue expulsion, closed os.
Diagnosis
  • Diagnostic Imaging: Transvaginal Ultrasound to confirm fetal viability and assess retained products of conception.
Treatment
  • Threatened: Conservative/expectant management, rest.
  • Incomplete/Missed: Medical management with Misoprostol or surgical Dilation and Curettage (D&C).
017 Bacterial Vaginosis (BV) >8 times
Polymicrobial dysbiosis characterized by a decrease in lactobacilli and overgrowth of anaerobes like Gardnerella vaginalis.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Thin, homogeneous gray/white discharge with a distinct fishy odor, usually without vaginal inflammation/erythema.
Diagnostic Criteria (Amsel Criteria)
  • Requires 3 of 4: Thin discharge, Vaginal pH test (>4.5), Positive Whiff test (10% KOH), Presence of Clue Cells on Wet mount microscopy.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Treatment: Oral or topical Metronidazole or Clindamycin.
018 Turner Syndrome (45, XO) >8 times
Chromosomal disorder in females caused by complete or partial absence of one X chromosome. Classic cause of primary amenorrhea.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Short stature, webbed neck (pterygium colli), broad shield chest, coarctation of aorta, and streak ovaries causing hypergonadotropic hypogonadism.
Diagnosis
  • Diagnostic Standard: Karyotype Analysis demonstrating 45, XO pattern.
Treatment
  • Growth Hormone therapy for height enhancement; Estrogen and Progesterone hormone replacement therapy to induce puberty and maintain bone density.
019 Ovarian Germ Cell Tumors >8 times
Neoplasms derived from germ cells, most common in young females and adolescents.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Subtypes & Features
  • Mature Cystic Teratoma (Dermoid): Benign, contains ectodermal elements (hair, teeth, sebaceous material).
  • Dysgerminoma: Malignant, analogous to male seminoma.
Tumor Markers
  • Dysgerminoma: Elevated LDH and Serum β-hCG.
  • Yolk Sac Tumor: Elevated AFP.
Treatment
  • Surgical excision (Ovarian cystectomy or Salpingo-oophorectomy) + Platinum-based chemotherapy (BEP regime) for malignant variants.
020 Combined Oral Contraceptive Pills (COCPs) >12 times
Estrogen-progestin medications used for pregnancy prevention, cycle control, and symptomatic relief of hormonal conditions.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Indications & Benefits
  • Contraception, dysmenorrhea, PCOS, endometriosis.
  • Protective against: Ovarian and Endometrial cancers.
Absolute Contraindications
  • History of VTE/DVT/PE, stroke, migraine with aura, active liver disease, breast cancer, age >35 and smoking ≥15 cigarettes/day.
Mechanism of Action
  • Suppresses LH surge (inhibits ovulation) and thickens cervical mucus.
021 Uterine Prolapse >8 times
Herniation and descent of the uterus into the vaginal canal due to weakness of the pelvic floor muscles and supporting ligaments.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Feeling of heaviness or "fullness" in the pelvis, sensation of sitting on a ball, and a visible or palpable vaginal bulge.
  • Exacerbated by prolonged standing or coughing; often associated with urinary incontinence or defecatory difficulty.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Step: Pelvic examination in the dorsal lithotomy or standing position using a speculum to assess stage/descent.
Treatment
  • 1st Line (Conservative): Kegel exercises (pelvic floor muscle training) for mild cases; supportive pessaries for non-surgical candidates.
  • Definitive Surgical: Vaginal Hysterectomy or Sacrocolpopexy / Sacrospinous ligament fixation.
022 Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) >10 times
Polymicrobial upper genital tract infection (endometritis, salpingitis, pelvic peritonitis) most commonly caused by Chlamydia trachomatis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Triad: Lower abdominal/pelvic pain + Purulent cervical discharge + Fever.
  • Physical Exam: Exceptional cervical motion tenderness (Chandelier sign) and adnexal tenderness.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Nucleic Acid Amplification Test (NAAT) for Chlamydia/Gonorrhea; Vaginal wet mount showing leukorrhea.
  • Diagnostic Imaging: Transvaginal Ultrasound (TVUS) to rule out tubo-ovarian abscess (TOA).
Treatment
  • 1st Line Outpatient: Single dose IM Ceftriaxone + Oral Doxycycline + Oral Metronidazole.
  • Inpatient/Severe: IV Cefotetan / Cefoxitin + Doxycycline OR IV Clindamycin + Gentamicin.
023 Fibroadenoma >10 times
Most common benign tumor of the female breast, composed of glandular and fibrous stromal tissue, frequent in young women (<30 years).
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Classic presentation: Painless, freely mobile, firm, rubbery solitary breast mass ("breast mouse").
  • Size may slightly enlarge during pregnancy or near the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Imaging: Targeted Breast Ultrasound (shows a well-circumscribed, oval, hypoechoic lesion).
  • Definitive Diagnosis: Core needle biopsy (if suspicious or expanding).
Treatment
  • 1st Line: Reassurance and observation/conservative monitoring for small (<2 cm) confirmed lesions.
  • Definitive / Surgical Excision: Indicated if rapidly growing, symptomatic, or >3 cm.
024 Sheehan's Syndrome >8 times
Ischemic necrosis of the hypertrophied anterior pituitary gland secondary to massive intrapartum or postpartum hemorrhage.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Presentation: Failure to lactate (agalactorrhea) + Amenorrhea/Oligomenorrhea following severe postpartum hemorrhage.
  • Features of panhypopituitarism: Fatigue, weight loss, loss of axillary/pubic hair, and hypotension.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Hormonal baseline panel (decreased Prolactin, FSH, LH, TSH, ACTH, GH).
  • Diagnostic Imaging: Pituitary MRI showing an empty or partially empty sella turcica in late stages.
Treatment
  • Definitive Management: Lifelong targeted hormone replacement therapy (Hydrocortisone first, followed by Levothyroxine, and estrogen/progesterone).
025 Asherman Syndrome >8 times
Acquired condition characterized by intra-uterine adhesions (synechiae) and scarring, typically following over-aggressive dilation and curettage (D&C).
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Secondary amenorrhea or hypomenorrhea, cyclic pelvic pain, and secondary infertility/recurrent pregnancy loss.
  • Negative response to progesterone withdrawal test.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Hysterosalpingography (HSG) or Saline Infusion Sonography showing filling defects.
  • Gold Standard Diagnostic Test: Hysteroscopy (directly visualizes fibrous intrauterine bands).
Treatment
  • Definitive Management: Hysteroscopic lysis of adhesions followed by post-operative high-dose Estrogen therapy and placement of an intrauterine balloon stent to prevent rescarring.
026 Partogram >6 times
Graphical record tracking key parameters of labor progress (cervical dilation, fetal station, contractions) to detect prolonged or obstructed labor early.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Utility
  • Monitors active phase of labor (starts at 5-6 cm dilation).
  • Includes Alert line (slow labor progress warning) and Action line (requires immediate intervention).
Diagnostic Findings
  • Protraction Disorder: Cervical dilation crossing to the right of the alert line.
  • Arrest of Labor: No cervical change for ≥4 hours despite adequate contractions, or ≥6 hours with inadequate contractions.
Treatment / Action
  • Inadequate Contractions: IV Oxytocin augmentation and artificial rupture of membranes (Amniotomy).
  • Obstructed Labor / Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD): Immediate Cesarean Section.
027 Amniocentesis >8 times
Invasive prenatal diagnostic procedure involving ultrasound-guided aspiration of amniotic fluid for genetic and fetal testing.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Indications
  • Confirming chromosomal abnormalities (e.g., Down syndrome), neural tube defects (AChE activity), or intrauterine fetal infections.
  • Preferred window: Performed between 15 to 20 weeks gestation.
Procedure & Diagnostic Tests
  • Performed using continuous Ultrasound guidance.
  • Diagnostic tests on fluid: Fetal Karyotyping, Chromosomal Microarray, and Alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) analysis.
Complications & Prophylaxis
  • Procedure-related pregnancy loss risk (~0.1 to 0.3%), amniotic fluid leakage, infection.
  • Administration of Anti-D Immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) post-procedure in Rh-negative unsensitized mothers.
028 Choriocarcinoma >8 times
A highly malignant epithelial tumor originating from trophoblastic tissue, commonly following a hydatidiform mole, normal pregnancy, or abortion.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Persistent post-molar or post-partum irregular vaginal bleeding, enlarged uterus, and signs of distant hematogenous metastasis.
  • Metastasis to lungs presents with hemoptysis and dyspnea ("cannonball" lung lesions).
Diagnosis
  • Initial & Diagnostic Test: Markedly elevated Serum Quantitative β-hCG levels.
  • Staging/Imaging: Chest CT scan or Pelvic Ultrasound / MRI. Tissue biopsy is contraindicated due to severe bleeding risk.
Treatment
  • 1st Line (Low Risk): Single-agent chemotherapy with Methotrexate or Actinomycin-D.
  • High Risk / Advanced: Multi-agent chemotherapy (EMA-CO regime). High cure rate even with metastases.
029 Mastitis >8 times
Acute regional infection/inflammation of breast tissue, most commonly occurring during the first few weeks of lactation, primarily caused by Staphylococcus aureus.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Unilateral painful, swollen, warm, erythematous wedge-shaped area of the breast.
  • Associated systemic symptoms: High fever, chills, and flu-like malaise.
Diagnosis
  • Diagnostic Method: Clinical diagnosis based on physical exam.
  • Targeted Ultrasound: Indicated if flucloxacillin treatment fails, to rule out a localized breast abscess (fluctuant mass).
Treatment
  • 1st Line Antibiotics: Oral penicillinase-resistant penicillins like Dicloxacillin, Flucloxacillin, or Cephalexin.
  • Essential Advice: Continued breastfeeding or active pumping to promote milk drainage.
030 Hyperemesis Gravidarum >8 times
Severe, intractable nausea and vomiting in early pregnancy leading to dehydration, electrolyte disturbances, and weight loss >5% of pre-pregnancy mass.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Persistent vomiting, orthostatic dizziness, dry mucous membranes, and muscle wasting.
  • Severe cases can lead to esophageal mucosal tears (Mallory-Weiss syndrome) or Wernicke encephalopathy due to thiamine deficiency.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Laboratory Tests: Urine Dipstick (showing ketonuria), Serum Electrolytes (hypokalemic, hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis).
  • Ultrasound: Rule out gestational trophoblastic disease or multiple gestation.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Antiemetics: Oral/IV Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6) alone or in combination with Doxylamine.
  • Severe/Inpatient: IV fluid replacement with normal saline, IV Ondansetron / Promethazine, and mandatory Thiamine (Vitamin B1) supplementation prior to glucose-containing fluids.
031 Breast Cancer Screening >8 times
Systematic screening protocols aimed at early detection of breast lesions in asymptomatic average and high-risk women.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Risk Factors
  • Screening evaluates microcalcifications, architecture distortion, or impalpable masses.
  • High-risk factors include BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations, strong family history, or prior chest radiation.
Diagnostic Screening Modalities
  • Standard Screening (>40 years): Biennial or annual screening Mammography.
  • Younger Women (<30–35 years) / Dense Breasts: Targeted Breast Ultrasound due to dense glandular tissue.
  • High-Risk Candidates: Contrast-Enhanced Breast MRI starting at age 30.
Definitive Diagnosis
  • Triple Assessment: Clinical exam + Imaging (Mammogram/US) + Core Needle Biopsy.
032 Tocolytics in Preterm Labor >8 times
Pharmacological agents used to suppress uterine contractions to delay preterm delivery by 48 hours, allowing time for corticosteroid administration.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Indications
  • Documented preterm labor between 24 and 34 weeks gestation with intact or ruptured membranes without intra-amniotic infection.
First-Line Tocolytic Drugs
  • Nifedipine: Oral calcium channel blocker (most widely used 1st-line drug due to low side-effect profile).
  • Indomethacin: NSAID (preferred <32 weeks; risk of premature closure of ductus arteriosus if used >48 hrs).
  • Terbutaline: Beta-2 agonist (used for rapid short-term acute relaxation; risk of maternal tachycardia).
  • Atosiban: Oxytocin receptor antagonist.
Absolute Contraindications
  • Chorioamnionitis, severe abruptio placentae, lethal fetal anomaly, or preeclampsia with severe features.
033 Magnesium Sulfate Toxicity >8 times
Iatrogenic toxicity occurring during IV MgSO4 infusion for eclampsia seizure prophylaxis or neuroprotection.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Sequence of Signs
  • 1st Sign (7–10 mEq/L): Loss of deep tendon reflexes (patellar reflex).
  • 2nd Sign (10–12 mEq/L): Somnolence, slurred speech, respiratory depression (<12 breaths/min).
  • Severe Toxicity (>15 mEq/L): Cardiac arrest and complete heart block.
Monitoring Protocols
  • Serial assessment of patellar reflexes, respiratory rate (≥12/min), and hourly urine output (≥30 mL/hr).
Management & Antidote
  • 1st Action: Immediately stop MgSO4 infusion.
  • Antidote: Slow IV administration of 10% Calcium Gluconate (1 gram over 10 minutes).
034 Trichomoniasis >8 times
Sexually transmitted vaginitis caused by the flagellated protozoan parasite Trichomonas vaginalis.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Signs
  • Malodorous, profuse, greenish-yellow frothy vaginal discharge accompanied by vulvar irritation and dyspareunia.
  • Physical Exam: Punctate cervical hemorrhages (Strawberry cervix).
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Vaginal pH test showing pH >4.5.
  • Diagnostic Test: Saline wet mount microscopy revealing motile, flagellated trichomonads.
  • Gold Standard: NAAT testing.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Pharmacotherapy: Single dose oral Metronidazole or Tinidazole.
  • Mandatory Protocol: Simultaneous treatment of all sexual partners to prevent reinfection.
035 Vaginal Candidiasis >8 times
Fungal vulvovaginal infection primarily caused by Candida albicans, often associated with recent antibiotic use, diabetes, or elevated estrogen levels.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Intense vulvovaginal pruritus, burning sensation, dysuria, and vulvar erythema/edema.
  • Classic Discharge: Thick, white, odorless "cottage cheese-like" discharge adherent to vaginal walls.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Vaginal pH test showing Normal pH (4.0–4.5).
  • Diagnostic Test: 10% KOH wet mount preparation showing pseudohyphae and budding yeast spores.
Treatment
  • 1st Line (Uncomplicated): Single oral dose of Fluconazole (150 mg) OR topical Clotrimazole / Miconazole vaginal suppositories.
  • In Pregnancy: Topical azoles only; oral fluconazole is contraindicated.
036 Ovarian Torsion >8 times
Surgical emergency involving partial or complete rotation of the ovary around its vascular pedicle (infundibulopelvic ligament), leading to ischemia and necrosis.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Sudden-onset, severe, sharp unilateral lower pelvic/abdominal pain, often accompanied by nausea and vomiting.
  • History of ovarian mass or cyst (e.g., dermoid cyst >5 cm). Tender adnexal mass on bimanual exam.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Diagnostic Imaging: Pelvic Color Doppler Ultrasound showing absent or decreased parenchymal arterial/venous blood flow and enlarged ovary.
Treatment
  • Definitive Management: Emergent Laparoscopy with detorsion and ovarian conservation (ovariopexy / cystectomy).
  • Non-Viable Ovary: Salpingo-oophorectomy if overt necrosis is present.
037 Imperforate Hymen >6 times
Congenital outflow tract obstruction resulting from failure of the hymeneal membrane to centralize and perforate during embryonic development.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Triad: Adolescent female with primary amenorrhea + Cyclic monthly lower abdominal pain + Normal secondary sexual characteristics.
  • Physical Exam: Introitus inspection reveals a bulging, tense, bluish membrane (hematocolpos).
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Clinical visual examination of the vulva.
  • Confirmatory Imaging: Pelvic Ultrasound showing blood accumulation in vagina and uterus (hematocolpos/hematometra).
Treatment
  • Definitive Surgical Treatment: Cruciate Hymenotomy (incision and drainage of blood with hymeneal tissue excision).
038 Antenatal Corticosteroid Therapy >8 times
Administration of corticosteroids to mothers at risk of preterm birth to accelerate fetal lung maturity and surfactant production.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Indications
  • All pregnant women between 24 and 34 weeks gestation who are at high risk of delivery within 7 days.
Standard Drug Regimens
  • Betamethasone: IM injection (12 mg, 2 doses given 24 hours apart).
  • Dexamethasone: IM injection (6 mg, 4 doses given 12 hours apart).
Clinical Benefits
  • Significantly reduces incidence of Neonatal Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS), Intraventricular Hemorrhage (IVH), and Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC).
039 LNG-IUS (Levonorgestrel Intrauterine System) >8 times
Progestin-releasing intrauterine device (e.g., Mirena) providing long-acting reversible contraception and endometrial suppression.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Indications
  • Highly effective contraception (>99% efficacy for up to 5–8 years).
  • 1st Line Medical Management: Idiopathic heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia), dysmenorrhea, and endometrial hyperplasia protection.
Mechanism of Action
  • Local endometrial atrophy, thickening of cervical mucus, and suppression of sperm motility.
Contraindications
  • Active pelvic infection/PID, unexplained vaginal bleeding, uterine anatomical distortion, or active breast cancer.
040 Twin Gestation >6 times
Multifetal pregnancy carrying higher risks of maternal and fetal complications, categorized by chorionicity and amnicity.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Classifications & Presentation
  • Chorionicity Assessment: Determined at 10–14 weeks via ultrasound ("T-sign" in Monochorionic vs "Twin peak / Lambda sign" in Dichorionic).
  • Most Common Delivery Presentation: Cephalic-Cephalic (~45%).
Diagnosis & Complications
  • Diagnostic Tool: Obstetric Ultrasound.
  • Complications: Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS in monochorionic twins), fetal growth restriction, IUGR, and preterm labor.
Delivery Guidelines
  • Cephalic-Cephalic: Vaginal delivery trial.
  • Non-Cephalic Twin A: Cesarean Section.
041 Rectovaginal Fistula >6 times
An epithelialized connection between the anterior wall of the rectum and the posterior wall of the vagina.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Involuntary passage of flatus or feculent discharge via the vagina.
  • Recurrent vaginitis, malodorous discharge, and severe perineal skin irritation. Often follows 3rd/4th degree obstetric perineal lacerations or prolonged obstructed labor.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Test: Direct visualization via Speculum examination and digital rectovaginal palpation.
  • Adjunct Diagnostics: Anovaginal Endosonography or Methylene blue tampons test if small/inapparent.
Treatment
  • Definitive Treatment: Surgical repair (Fistulotomy / Endorectal advancement flap) typically performed 3–6 months after injury to allow inflammation to subside.

Infection of the genital tract occurring at any time between rupture of membranes or labor up to 6 weeks postpartum.

Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Presentation: Postpartum fever (>38.0°C) + Foul-smelling lochia + Uterine tenderness/subinvolution.

  • Initial Assessment: Clinical exam; CBC with differential (leukocytosis), Blood cultures, and endometrial cultures.

043 Ultrasound Dating Scan >8 times
First-trimester ultrasound evaluation performed to establish accurate gestational age, confirm intrauterine pregnancy, and check fetal viability.

  • Optimal Timing: Performed between 10 and 14 weeks gestation.
  • Parameter: Measurement of fetal Crown-Rump Length (CRL).

  • CRL Precision: Most accurate measurement for dating pregnancy (margin of error ±5–7 days).

  • Integrated with Nuchal Translucency (NT) scan and serum markers (PAPP-A, free β-hCG) for aneuploidy screening.

Malpresentation where the fetal buttocks or lower extremities present first into the maternal pelvis (Frank, Complete, or Footling).
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features
  • Palpation of hard round fetal head in the uterine fundus on Leopold maneuvers; soft irregular structures on vaginal examination.

045 Bishop Score >8 times
Pre-induction scoring system used to predict the likelihood of successful labor induction based on cervical readiness.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Parameters Assessed (5 Variables)
  • Cervical Dilation, Cervical Effacement, Cervical Consistency (Soft/Medium/Firm), Cervical Position (Anterior/Intermediate/Posterior), and Fetal Station relative to ischial spines.
Score Interpretation
  • Favorable Cervix (Score ≥8): High likelihood of successful induction (similar to spontaneous labor).
  • Unfavorable Cervix (Score ≤6): Requires cervical ripening prior to induction.
Treatment / Interventions
  • Unfavorable Cervix: Cervical ripening with Dinoprostone (PGE2) or Misoprostol (PGE1), or mechanical balloon catheter.
  • Favorable Cervix: Direct administration of IV Oxytocin or Amniotomy.
046 Intrauterine Contraceptive Device (IUCD) Contraindications >8 times
Strict safety guidelines defining absolute and relative medical contraindications for Copper or Progestin IUD placement.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Absolute Contraindications
  • Pregnancy, active pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) or purulent cervicitis within the last 3 months, unexplained abnormal uterine bleeding, uterine cavity anomalies (bicornuate, large fibroids), active pelvic malignancy.
  • Copper IUD specific: Wilson's disease or severe copper allergy.
Diagnosis / Pre-insertion Assessment
  • Mandatory Pre-requisites: Urine Pregnancy Test, bimanual pelvic exam, and STI screening (Chlamydia/Gonorrhea) if high risk.

  • Perforation / Dislodgement: Confirm position via Pelvic Ultrasound; perform hysteroscopic or laparoscopic removal if embedded outside cavity.
047 Primary Amenorrhea Evaluation >8 times
Diagnostic algorithm for evaluating absence of menses by age 13 without secondary sexual characteristics, or age 15 with normal growth/secondary development.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Diagnostic Step 1 & 2
  • Initial Step: Physical exam to confirm breast development (estrogen presence) and Pelvic Ultrasound to confirm presence or absence of uterus.
Diagnostic Lab Workup
  • Uterus Present: Measure Serum FSH/LH levels (differentiates Hypergonadotropic [e.g., Turner 45,XO] vs Hypogonadotropic amenorrhea).
  • Uterus Absent: Perform Karyotyping analysis (differentiates Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome [46,XX] vs Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome [46,XY]).
Treatment
  • Targeted according to etiology: Estrogen replacement (Turner), gonadectomy after puberty (AIS), or surgical correction of structural tract obstruction.
048 Anorexia Nervosa (Gynecological Focus) >8 times
Functional hypothalamic amenorrhea caused by severe calorie restriction, low body weight, and stress-induced disruption of hypothalamic signaling.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Pathophysiology & Features
  • Suppression of pulsatile GnRH secretion from the hypothalamus due to low leptin and elevated cortisol levels.
  • Leads to hypogonadotropic hypogonadal state: Low FSH, low LH, low Estrogen.
  • Presents with secondary amenorrhea, cold intolerance, lanugo hair, and osteopenia/osteoporosis.
Diagnosis
  • Lab Testing: Low/normal Serum FSH and LH, low Estradiol, normal Prolactin/TSH.
  • Diagnostic Imaging: DEXA scan to evaluate bone mineral density.
Treatment
  • 1st Line Definitive Treatment: Nutritional rehabilitation, weight restoration, and psychotherapy (restores GnRH pulsatility).
  • Note: OCPs do not reliably improve bone density in functional hypothalamic amenorrhea without weight gain.
049 Kallmann Syndrome >8 times
Genetic disorder characterized by failure of GnRH-secreting neurons and olfactory neurons to migrate from the olfactory placode to the hypothalamus.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Clinical Features & Triad
  • Classic Triad: Primary amenorrhea + Absent secondary sexual characteristics + Anosmia / Hyposmia (loss of smell).
  • Associated defects: Cleft lip/palate, renal agenesis.
Diagnosis
  • Initial Lab Test: Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism pattern: Low Serum GnRH, FSH, LH, and Estradiol.
  • Diagnostic Assessment: Formal Olfactory testing (UPSIT) + Brain MRI showing absence or hypoplasia of olfactory bulbs.
Treatment
  • Secondary Sex Characteristics / Puberty: Hormone replacement therapy with Estrogen and Progesterone.
  • Fertility Induction: Exogenous Pulsatile GnRH or Gonadotropins (FSH/LH).
050 Vaginal Discharge pH Differential >8 times
High-yield comparative diagnostic testing parameter used to rapidly differentiate common causes of vaginitis.
▼ Expand Detailed Clinical Information
Diagnostic pH Values
  • Normal Vaginal pH: 3.8 – 4.5 (maintained by Lactobacillus lactic acid production).
  • Vaginal Candidiasis: Normal pH (4.0 – 4.5).
  • Bacterial Vaginosis (BV): Elevated pH (>4.5).
  • Trichomoniasis: Elevated pH (>4.5).
Confirmatory Diagnostic Tests
  • BV: 10% KOH Whiff test (positive fishy odor) + Clue cells on wet mount.
  • Trichomoniasis: Motile flagellated protozoa on wet mount.
  • Candidiasis: Pseudohyphae / spores on 10% KOH prep.
1st Line Treatments
  • BV & Trichomoniasis: Metronidazole.
  • Candidiasis: Fluconazole or topical azoles.
